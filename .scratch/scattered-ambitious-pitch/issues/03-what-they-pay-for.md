## Question

What do people adjacent to the scattered ambitious person already pay for, keep paying for, and recommend to a peer?

The buyer in this effort is the person, paying for themselves. Look at self-pay productivity products they already might hire: task managers, calendars with opinions, focus tools, body doubling, and tools aimed at attention trouble.

Use primary sources only: pricing pages, company filings, first-party retention or referral claims, and studies that publish their own methods. If a price, retention rate, or referral claim is not in a primary source, omit the number and say it was not found.

Read `CONTEXT.md`. Report price bands that are actually published, what the payment is for, and any sourced evidence that people stay or tell a peer. Do not pick a business model.

## Answer

A scattered ambitious self-payer already spreads money across a wide band: free tiers and low subscriptions ($3–$12/month) through mid-tier daily planners ($17–$34/month) up to premium AI schedulers ($19–$29/seat/month), plus one-time task apps around $10–$35 per Apple platform. Referral and peer-recommendation mechanics exist, but published subscription retention rates were not found.
