## Question

For a scattered ambitious person in goal neglect — an intention already formed, possibly still recallable, that has stopped governing action — when, why, and under what cues does an external record actually get used so the intention governs behavior again?

Read `CONTEXT.md` and [Name the spine](04-name-the-spine.md). Organizing thoughts is compensation, not the spine. A capture session can outrun the person. The open question is retrieval.

Use primary sources: prospective-memory cue research (event-based and time-based), implementation intentions, intention offloading, and studies of whether reminders and external stores are actually consulted. Follow each claim to the source that owns it. Mark what you could only reach as an abstract.

Speak to three moments, and say what the sources support in each:

- while the person is still capturing, when a related past record appears beside the thought
- at the moment of action, when an intention has to change what they do next
- at a later review, when they go looking

Say which of those moments the sources treat as a cause of the intention governing action, and which they treat as a side activity that can fail while the person still feels they "have notes." Do not propose a product.

## Answer

Sources treat the **moment of action** as where an intention starts governing behavior again: a cue must retrieve the intended action when the person could do it next — via focal event cues, time monitoring, implementation-intention links, or a consulted external reminder placed as a perceptual trigger. Merely capturing, storing, or resurfacing related material can leave the person with notes they still do not consult or act on; later review is voluntary retrieval, not automatic PM.
