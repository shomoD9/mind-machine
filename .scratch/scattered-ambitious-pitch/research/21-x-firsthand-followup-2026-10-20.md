# X follow-up: direct accounts from Chrome

Five original X posts were opened and read on 20 October 2026 through the user's existing Chrome session. This supplements the [initial social report](16-social-problem-evidence-2026-10-19.md), whose web-only X access failed. No posts, replies, likes, bookmarks, follows, or account settings were changed.

## Method and limits

Searched X Latest for second-brain organization/maintenance, note graveyards, forgotten notes, and WhatsApp self-capture. Recent results included substantial app promotion, general advice, irrelevant matches, and an explicitly automated promotional account. Those were excluded from the firsthand register. An additional `until:2025-01-01` search recovered earlier accounts; they are historical experiences, not current product capability evidence. No inference is made from engagement counts.

Five selected original post pages supplied visible full publication dates. Dates below are transcribed from those pages rather than inferred from post IDs or relative age. Displayed times are omitted because the source's timezone was not established. Quotes are kept short; the post text is otherwise paraphrased.

This is a small purposive sample. Identity, affiliation, authenticity, and the reported usage were not independently verified. A lack of a visible pitch does not prove absence of commercial motivation. These posts corroborate situations, without estimating their prevalence or validating the proposed keyboard.

## Original-post register

### X01 — Trying note apps but returning to WhatsApp

- **Source:** [@pr3cious_ly, original post](https://x.com/pr3cious_ly/status/1859681394492092629).
- **Visible publication date:** 22 November 2024.
- **Observed account:** The author says that after downloading and trying multiple note apps, they return to a WhatsApp group containing only themselves and prefer it for notes. A reply also reports using self-messaging for notes.
- **Short quote:** “i always default to that WhatsApp group with myself.”
- **Implication:** A replacement must beat an established capture habit. This post does not report a retrieval failure or ask for semantic search.

### X02 — Long-used Obsidian collection without trusted value

- **Source:** [@piotryordanov, original post](https://x.com/piotryordanov/status/1866480940685639701).
- **Visible publication date:** 10 December 2024.
- **Observed account:** The author reports long-term Obsidian use without finding a workflow that brings value. Their desired outcomes include safe knowledge sharing, trustworthy retrieval, and logical organization.
- **Short quote:** “I haven't cracked a way that brings me value.”
- **Implication:** Longevity and storage alone do not settle usefulness. The post names aspirations; it supplies no accuracy or maintenance-time measurements.

### X03 — A working multi-app stack is counterevidence

- **Source:** [@viajesubmarino, original post](https://x.com/viajesubmarino/status/1871269565835219188).
- **Visible publication date:** 24 December 2024.
- **Observed account:** The author describes Google Keep for quick capture and sync, Obsidian for organized thoughts, automation tools, and a separate publishing stack. The account presents these tools positively.
- **Implication:** Multiple apps can have useful distinct roles. This supports comparing the proposed whole-day experience against the person's actual workflow rather than assuming consolidation is always better. It is a self-reported stack, not observed success or retention data.

### X04 — Saved notes can sit outside the person's routine

- **Source:** [@ihrtpjimin, original post](https://x.com/ihrtpjimin/status/2111103290201293269).
- **Visible publication date:** 16 October 2026.
- **Observed account:** The author wants a thread for books they intend to read because they seldom open their notes app except for a specific existing need.
- **Implication:** A collection can be available yet rarely encountered. The post does not establish that random reminders, contextual suggestions, or a keyboard would be welcome. Do not infer what private material the notes contain.

### X05 — Fear of losing a thought motivates capture

- **Source:** [@l0vesexdrugb0mb, original post](https://x.com/l0vesexdrugb0mb/status/2111399074851140081).
- **Visible publication date:** 17 October 2026.
- **Observed account:** The author considers resuming notes-app journaling because there is much they want to discuss and fear forgetting.
- **Implication:** Immediate offloading has a benefit even before retrieval. This is an intention to journal, not evidence of adopting or abandoning a second-brain product.

## Effect on the product argument

The direct X material corroborates familiar capture habits, a desire for trusted retrieval, fear of losing thoughts, and the problem of a collection outside one's routine. It also supplies a positive multi-app counterexample. It leaves the core design decisions unchanged: capture without filing; recover recognizable originals; make resurfacing optional; offer action on task-like thoughts explicitly. This sample supplies no evidence to choose a default keyboard, a resurfacing trigger, or a pricing tier.

The combined social evidence now consists of **24 initially opened discussion pages plus five directly opened original X posts**. The initially accessed Forte mirror remains context only. Several additional search-result posts were inspected for relevance but are excluded from the substantive count.
